My Ranch

Map your ranch and see how many cattle it can carry.

Find your place on the satellite map and draw each pasture. The tool measures the acres, looks up the USDA soil survey's forage estimate for each pasture, and turns it into a carrying capacity in animal units — then checks it against your planned herd, the current Drought Monitor, and the AgriLife 2026 cow-calf budget.

Your ranch on the map

Start here: search for your ranch or use My location, zoom in until you can see your fences, then press Draw pasture.

Pastures

For rangeland, forage comes from the USDA-NRCS soil survey: its estimate of total plant growth per acre in a normal year, weighted by the soils inside each pasture. Improved pasture, wheat and hay meadows usually have no soil-survey number — enter your own estimate (your county agent can help).

Grazing days by pasture

How many days each pasture can carry your planned herd, using the same forage and harvest-efficiency numbers as the capacity estimate. Use the arrows to set the order you'd graze them in.

Water, pens & gates

Use the buttons above the map to drop a water point, pens, a gate or a feeder, then click where it goes. Water points let the pasture list show how far the middle of each pasture is from the nearest water.

Save, share & print

Your map is saved automatically in this browser only — nothing is sent to Texas A&M. To move it to another device or give it to your agent, download a file and open it there.

Opens Google Earth files (KML/KMZ), GeoJSON, or a zipped shapefile from your agent or FSA office.

Forage estimates: USDA-NRCS Soil Survey (SSURGO) through Soil Data Access, “range production, normal year” weighted by soil area and component share. Drought status: U.S. Drought Monitor (National Drought Mitigation Center, USDA, NOAA), current week. Stocking rate method: AgriLife Extension, Stocking Rate: The Key Grazing Management Decision. Budget benchmark: AgriLife Extension 2026 District 3 (Rolling Plains) cow-calf enterprise budget. Satellite imagery © Esri and its partners; search © OpenStreetMap contributors. These are planning estimates, not a range inspection — walk your pastures and check with your county agent or NRCS office before setting a stocking rate.
